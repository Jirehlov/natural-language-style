# Chinese translationese and English-shaped clause diagnostics

Use this reference whenever drafting, rewriting, translating into, or diagnosing Chinese prose. The goal is native Chinese information packaging. Treat the patterns as diagnostics, not word bans.

## 1. Abstract or inanimate subjects with human predicates

English readily makes abstract nouns and inanimate entities grammatical subjects. Chinese can also do this, especially for genuine causation, institutional metonymy, source attribution, and deliberate literary personification. Problems arise when the English subject slot is copied into Chinese and the predicate semantically requires a human experiencer or agent.

High-risk predicates include deliberate choice, cognition, perception, speech, moral response, and interpersonal action: `决定`, `选择`, `拒绝`, `接受`, `相信`, `记住`, `看见`, `回应`, `服从`, `承担`, `邀请`, `提醒`, `要求`, `希望`, `拥抱`, `面对`, `庆祝`.

Diagnostic question: **Who can literally do this verb here?**

If the answer is a person or institution that the sentence has hidden, restore that actor. If no actor exists, recast the relation as cause, evidence, condition, purpose, content, result, or topic-comment. **Also inspect covert agency when the abstract expression is not the grammatical subject.**

### Semantic agency can be concealed without an abstract grammatical subject

Check what **the sentence implies** about initiative and decision-making before accepting it. A human grammatical subject can coexist with a covert abstract agent in an adverbial, nominal complement, passive phrase, causative frame, or possessive:

| Hidden mechanism | High-risk wording | Literal repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guidance as an adverbial | `在理论的引领下，研究者调整了解释。` | `研究者根据理论中的论点调整了解释。` |
| Command as a noun modifier | `按照框架提出的要求，团队重新分类。` | `团队按照框架中的分类标准重新分类。` |
| Evidence as an adviser | `受数据提醒，研究者复查结论。` | `研究者根据数据复查结论。` |
| Abstraction as decision-maker | `团队把选择交给了方法。` | `团队根据方法的评估标准作出选择。` |
| Intent imported into an abstract purpose | `人们响应历史的召唤。` | `人们根据当时的现实情况采取行动。` (only if the source supports that motivation) |

An abstract expression is suspicious whenever the full proposition suggests that it wants something, consciously acts, chooses on someone's behalf, delivers commands, judges people, or deliberately changes their conduct. **Repositioning the abstract noun does not remove the semantic claim.** Reconstruct the actor, evidence, condition, rule, constraint, or outcome. Do not invent agency or causality that the evidence never established.

Do not prohibit all abstract causation. `气温升高加快了蒸发` can describe physical causality without any intention. For documentary attribution, prefer a verifiable description such as `报告列明` or `报告中的数据表明` and avoid giving a document independent judgment or intent. Under a user's explicitly requested literary personification, follow that requested genre; otherwise default to literal prose.

Examples:

- English-shaped: `科学决策……让模型参数和统计结果决定下一步行动。`
- Rebuilt: `研究人员审查数据后，根据模型参数和统计结果决定下一步行动。`

- English-shaped: `这个张力提醒我们……`
- Rebuilt: `把这两处材料放在一起看，可以看出……`

- English-shaped: `这个框架要求我们把两者分开。`
- Rebuilt: `按照这个框架分析时，需要把两者分开。`

Legitimate causal or institutional subjects include `高温会加快蒸发` and `法院裁定驳回上诉`. For evidence attribution, prefer `报告显示样本量不足` when the report actually presents that finding; avoid formulations that assign independent intention or judgment to a document.

## 2. Restrict 它 and avoid abstract referential pronouns

Apply the user's stricter-than-ordinary Chinese rule:

1. **`它` / `它的`: concrete entities only.** Permit only for a clear, specific object or animal when a pronoun really improves readability. Do not use for a theory, idea, framework, proposal, policy, argument, evidence, result, event, goal, process, category, abstract system, or previous proposition. For institutions or systems with ambiguous concreteness, prefer the entity's name or type rather than treating `它` as a default.
2. **First choice for an abstract referent: omit the pronoun.** Keep the same subject across consecutive predicates when unambiguous. If several consecutive short sentences repeat the same subject, first consider combining compatible predicates; omission alone can leave an abrupt chain of fragments. Do not delete a needed subject if the result becomes unclear or violates the one-subject requirement.
3. **Second choice: repeat or specify the noun.** Prefer `该理论`, `这项政策`, `上述结论`, `方案的实施周期`, `证据的范围` and similar explicit noun phrases. These are identifiable nominal expressions, not bare anaphoric pronouns.
4. **Last resort: `其`.** Use only when an abstract pronoun is indispensable, the antecedent is unique, and a formal `其` is idiomatic. Avoid frequent `其`; it can read like translationese or legal boilerplate. Never blindly change `它` to `其` or say `其希望/选择/邀请` to hide an agency error.
5. **Audit other bare anaphoric pronouns for abstracts.** `这`, `那`, `这些`, `这一点` and similar standalone references can copy English `this/that`; use explicit noun phrases where needed. A determiner with a named head noun (`这一结论`, `该方法`) is preferable to a standalone abstract pronoun.
6. **Check entire paragraph after edits.** Pronoun reference can become ambiguous when antecedents are inserted, deleted, or reordered. Rebuild nearby sentences rather than adding a chain of `其` to repair English-style continuity.

Examples:

- Problem: `这项政策分三步实施。它的第二阶段预计持续半年。` → Preferred: `这项政策分三步实施。第二阶段预计持续半年。` (the period and shared context make the referent clear)
- Problem: `该方案已公布。它的执行成本较高。` → Preferred: `该方案已公布。执行该方案的成本较高。`
- Problem: `这套理论有三个前提。它要求我们接受这些前提。` → Preferred: `这套理论包含三个前提。研究者需要先检验这些前提。` **Only use the second sentence when checking premises is the supported action; otherwise omit it rather than fabricating an obligation.**
- Problem: `证据已更新。这表明我们需要重做实验。` → Preferred only if justified: `根据新增证据，研究者有理由重新评估实验设计。` (verify the inference; do not make the result mechanically 'speak')
- Legitimate rare `其`: `该模型包含三项关键参数。其适用范围见附录。` Still prefer `模型的适用范围见附录。` when equally clear.

### Further diagnostics

Audit each `它` for a concrete antecedent, not just a plausible noun. `它的` referring to the preceding idea is a particularly common English-calque pattern. If substitution changes a subject, rerun the one-subject check and the semantic-agency test. Reserve `其` for genuine necessity and explicit nonagentive use.


## 3. Heavy pre-nominal modification and `的` chains

English can place substantial conceptual structure inside noun phrases. Chinese becomes difficult when events and argument relations are packed into a long `NP + 的 + NP` chain.

Warning signs:

- several nested `的` relations before the head noun;
- an event expressed as a noun phrase even though a finite clause would be clearer;
- multiple participants whose semantic roles must be reconstructed by the reader.

Repair by promoting one or more embedded events into clauses. Reorder by time, cause, actor, or topic.

Example:

- Dense: `对已有调查结果的进一步分析所带来的对消费者行为的重新理解`
- Clearer: `研究人员进一步分析已有调查结果，并据此重新理解消费者行为。`

Do not count every `的`; short ordinary possessives and established noun phrases are natural Chinese.

## 4. Nominalization and light verbs

English formal prose often packages actions as nouns. Chinese usually reads more directly when the action is a verb.

Common repairs:

- `进行分析` → `分析`
- `作出决定` → `决定`
- `实现转变` → `转变` / specify who changed what
- `产生影响` → `影响`
- `提供支持` → `支持` when no technical distinction is needed
- `进行讨论` → `讨论`
- `完成对……的评估` → `评估……`

Keep nominalizations when they are established technical terms, legal categories, or when the noun itself is the topic being discussed.

## 5. Source-driven passive voice

English and Chinese distribute passive constructions differently. A model may carry English passive voice into Chinese because the source clause is passive.

Check `被`, `受到`, `得到`, `遭到`, and passive-like `为……所……`. Keep them when the affected entity is the point, the agent is unknown or intentionally backgrounded, or the genre convention requires it. Otherwise test:

- active voice with the real agent;
- topic-comment order;
- an agentless Chinese clause that describes the result directly.

Examples:

- Source-shaped: `这个问题被许多研究者讨论。`
- Natural alternatives: `许多研究者讨论过这个问题。` / `关于这个问题，已有不少研究。`

## 6. Causative calques

English `make`, `let`, `allow`, and `enable` often tempt Chinese output into `使/使得/让/允许 + object + verb` even when the relation is not literal causation or permission.

Ask what relation actually holds:

- cause → state the cause and effect;
- evidence → `根据/从……可以看出`;
- basis for choice → `根据……决定/选择`;
- purpose → `目的是……`;
- condition → `在……情况下……`;
- opportunity/capability → state the capability directly.

Avoid making abstract evidence or concepts look like agents that command human choices.

## 7. Stacked prepositional frames

English noun-heavy prose can produce Chinese strings such as `基于……，通过……，在……层面，对于……而言……`. Too many fronted frames postpone the main verb and make the sentence bureaucratic.

Repair by finding the main action first. Keep only the relation needed for interpretation. Turn the rest into separate clauses if necessary.

Example:

- Heavy: `基于对该问题的分析，在实践层面上，我们可以通过对流程的调整来实现效率的提升。`
- Direct: `分析这个问题后，可以调整流程，提高效率。`

## 8. Over-explicit cohesion

Chinese often leaves discourse relations to adjacency, chronology, shared topic, and lexical continuity. English and translated prose may mark more relations overtly.

Audit:

`因此`, `所以`, `然而`, `但是`, `此外`, `另外`, `与此同时`, `从而`, `进而`, `这意味着`, `换言之`, `也就是说`.

Keep a connective when it resolves a real ambiguity or expresses a relation the reader needs. Remove it when sentence order already makes the relation obvious. This diagnostic works together with the skill's separate gates for concession, persistence, and correction.

Crucially, deleting a connective must not turn the paragraph into a pile of loosely adjacent or abrupt short statements. Inspect the link between every adjacent sentence and the passage's overall rhythm; when shared-subject content can be integrated naturally, rebuild the sentence instead of adding periods. Apply the strict one-subject rule and standards-compliant semicolon gate in `chinese-sentence-coherence.md`. A comma is not an acceptable boundary between independent clauses with different subjects under this house style.

## 9. Coordination and list packaging

Translated Chinese can over-coordinate noun phrases or verb phrases with `和/以及/与/、`, producing long inventory-like sentences. Do not force several different relations into one grammatical list merely because English coordinates them.

Split items when they have different roles, time frames, or causal status. Keep lists when the items really form one set.

Do not use a semicolon merely because list items have different subjects. First confirm true clause-level parallelism, structural layering, or a qualified enumeration under GB/T 15834-2011.

## 10. English-style evaluative predicates and abstract conclusions

Watch for abstract subjects followed by broad evaluative predicates that postpone the actual information:

- `现实是残酷的`
- `复杂性是开放的`
- `这个问题是深刻的`
- `其意义是巨大的`

These can be grammatical Chinese but often function as empty staging in LLM prose. Replace them with the concrete condition, consequence, or observation when that is what the paragraph needs.

## 11. Clause-rebuild workflow

When local edits keep producing awkward Chinese, stop editing word by word. Rebuild the sentence:

1. Write down the proposition in plain semantic roles: actor/experiencer, action/state, patient, cause, evidence, time, condition, result.
2. Decide the Chinese topic from the discourse, not from the English grammatical subject.
3. Omit a recoverable subject when normal Chinese permits it.
4. Express actions as verbs and events as clauses unless a noun is genuinely needed.
5. Mark only discourse relations that add information.
6. Read the result aloud. If the reader must reverse-engineer an English sentence to understand it, rebuild again.
7. Confirm there is one subject per authored sentence (subject changes require separate sentences or a licensed semicolon), and confirm that this sentence has a clear role in the paragraph.
8. Scan the paragraph for a string of clipped sentences, especially repeated subjects and one-fact-per-period sentences. Combine compatible same-subject predicates without inventing relations; retain necessary short sentences. After any insert/delete, reread the entire affected argument.
8. Check implied abstract agency even if the grammatical subject is human or omitted; remove any covert personification from the rebuilt meaning.
9. Search for `它/它的/其` and bare references to abstract topics. Prefer explicit nouns or omission; use `其` for an abstract antecedent only when unavoidable.

## Research basis

These diagnostics are consistent with corpus and translation research, including:

- Hu, Li & Kübler (2018), *Detecting Syntactic Features of Translated Chinese*: translated Chinese showed increased use of determiners, pronouns in subject position, `NP + 的` modifiers, and several coordination patterns; syntactic features alone distinguished translated from original Chinese with high accuracy.
- Li, Langlais & Jin (2017), *Translating Implicit Discourse Connectives Based on Cross-lingual Annotation and Alignment*: Chinese discourse relations are often implicit, while English translation tends to make connectives explicit.
- Wang et al. (2023), *PROSE*: Chinese is a pronoun-dropping language, creating a systematic contrast with English.
- Kong & Macken (2025), *Decoding Machine Translationese in English-Chinese News*: machine-translated Chinese, including LLM output, was strongly distinguishable from original Chinese; increased adversative conjunction use was among the reported patterns.
- Ma et al. (2026), *Bidirectional Chinese and English Passive Sentences Dataset for Machine Translation*: MT systems were influenced by source-language voice and tended to preserve passive voice more than human translators.
- Li (2026), *A Linguistic Comparison of Chinese and English*: discusses the long-standing subject-prominence/topic-prominence contrast as one dimension of Chinese-English typological comparison.

Use these findings as diagnostic support, not as a claim that every occurrence of a listed structure is unnatural or AI-generated.
